Cross-Country Vinted Search: Comparing 5 Markets Without Burning Sessions

Part 2/4 of a developer series about turning Vinted scraping into scheduled reseller monitoring.

Disclosure: I built Vinted Smart Scraper on Apify. Some Apify links may use my referral code.

In Part 1, I showed why a Vinted monitor should track what changes, not just what exists. This part is about a different problem:

What happens when the same query behaves differently across countries?

I ran this live on June 4, 2026 with Vinted Smart Scraper.

🧨 The mistake I almost made

The first tempting version of this feature was simple:

scrape 5 countries -> pick cheapest -> pick most expensive -> call it arbitrage

That looks good in a screenshot, but it is not how reselling works. A nike air force 1 listing in France and a nike air force 1 listing in Germany can differ by:

  • Size — Why it matters: EUR 35 and EUR 45 are not the same market
  • Condition — Why it matters: "Good" can hide heavy wear
  • Photos — Why it matters: Some listings are underpriced because they look bad
  • Seller reliability — Why it matters: A cheap item from a weak seller is not always an opportunity
  • Shipping — Why it matters: Country spread can disappear after fees
  • Demand — Why it matters: A high listing price does not mean buyers pay it

So I do not want a fake "profit bot". I want a country radar:

Where should I spend my monitoring budget today?

That is the correct job for CROSS_COUNTRY.

🌍 The input

{
  "mode": "CROSS_COUNTRY",
  "query": "nike air force 1",
  "countries": ["fr", "de", "es", "it", "nl"],
  "condition": ["very_good", "good"],
  "priceMin": 20,
  "priceMax": 120,
  "sortBy": "newest_first",
  "maxItems": 15,
  "includePhotos": false,
  "maxConcurrency": 1,
  "requestDelayMs": 500
}

I intentionally kept it small:

  • 5 countries — Why: Current stability cap
  • 15 max items — Why: 3 items per country
  • includePhotos: false — Why: Smaller output
  • maxConcurrency: 1 — Why: Slow but clean for a proof run

✅ Live result

  • Run ID — Value: 55PiTkUKZneTJo8Ep
  • Dataset ID — Value: 9I2fifqjEv9sQBmrg
  • Status — Value: SUCCEEDED
  • Duration — Value: 86s
  • Dataset rows emitted — Value: 1
  • Free-tier estimated charge — Value: $0.022

Why only one row? Because CROSS_COUNTRY emits a comparison object instead of 15 individual listing rows. That output shape is intentional. If the Actor emitted every country listing as a normal row, a small five-country radar would quickly become noisy:

  • 15 item rows — What you get: Raw listings from all countries | Problem: Harder to read in a webhook
  • 5 country rows — What you get: One row per market | Problem: Better, but still requires aggregation
  • 1 comparison row — What you get: Best buy/sell country + spread + table | Problem: Useful for scheduled decisions

For a recurring monitor, I care less about dumping every listing and more about answering:

Did the country spread become interesting enough to open a deeper monitor?

🧪 What the run actually proved

The useful part is not "Germany is profitable". The useful part is:

  • France median was 30 — What I can say: France looked cheaper in this small sample | What I cannot say: Every French AF1 listing is cheap
  • Germany median was 50 — What I can say: Germany looked higher in this sample | What I cannot say: German buyers will pay more
  • Spread was 47.4% — What I can say: The query is worth deeper monitoring | What I cannot say: Net resale profit is 47.4%
  • Five countries finished successfully — What I can say: The workflow handled country redirects | What I cannot say: It will always finish at the same speed
  • One dataset row was emitted — What I can say: Schedule output is compact | What I cannot say: Internal scraping was only one request

This distinction matters because a reseller does not need another messy spreadsheet. They need a short list of where to look next.

⚙️ Why cross-country scraping is harder than it looks

Vinted is not one global marketplace with one clean catalog. It behaves more like a group of country-specific markets that share branding but do not always behave the same way from a scraper perspective.

  • Country redirects — Practical effect: A request can land on the wrong country if session handling is weak
  • Different localized pages — Practical effect: The same query can expose slightly different metadata
  • Anti-bot variance — Practical effect: One country can be easy while another slows the whole run
  • Ranking drift — Practical effect: newest_first today is not the same as tomorrow
  • Result caps — Practical effect: A high maxItems across countries can multiply quickly
  • Currency/fees/shipping — Practical effect: EUR price comparison is only the first layer

That is why I kept this test at:

5 countries
3 items per country
1 emitted comparison row
86 seconds total

It is not the maximum possible configuration. It is a clean proof that the workflow can survive a small scheduled country radar.

⚠️ The hidden trap

The dangerous version of this feature would be:

"Here is a profitable arbitrage gap."

That would be sloppy. The safer version is:

"Here is a price spread from a small sample. Validate shipping, sizes, condition, fees, and actual sell-through before trusting it."

  • Country-level price spread — Still missing: Shipping and buyer behavior
  • Median/min/max — Still missing: Listing quality
  • 5-country snapshot — Still missing: Time-series confidence
  • Query-level comparison — Still missing: Exact product matching

🧭 The decision loop I would use

I would not schedule CROSS_COUNTRY as the final monitor. I would use it as step one:

cross-country radar -> pick promising country -> sell-through tracker -> exact item watchlist

Example:

  • 1 — Mode: CROSS_COUNTRY | Goal: Find which markets are unusually cheap/expensive
  • 2 — Mode: SELL_THROUGH_TRACKER | Goal: Check if inventory actually disappears
  • 3 — Mode: PRICE_TRACK | Goal: Watch exact URLs that are close to a buy decision
  • 4 — Mode: Webhook/Telegram/CSV | Goal: Push only useful signals

The important part is that every step narrows the search space. That is how you avoid turning a reseller tool into a bill generator.

💸 Cost guardrail

The live proof run emitted 1 row. At the Free pricing available when I ran the test:

$0.020 / Actor start
$0.002 / result row

That makes the live run roughly:

$0.020 + (1 * $0.002) = $0.022

Important nuance:

maxItems controls how much the Actor is allowed to inspect
dataset rows control the result-row charge

So a compact comparison output is useful for schedules, but you still need sane maxItems and country caps. My default:

  • First manual test — Suggested cap: 15 | Why: Fast proof
  • Daily radar — Suggested cap: 100 | Why: Enough signal, still controlled
  • Weekly scan — Suggested cap: 300 | Why: Wider sample without making it permanent
  • Always-on high frequency — Suggested cap: Avoid | Why: Use sell-through or exact watchlists instead

🔁 How I would schedule it

Use this weekly or daily:

{
  "mode": "CROSS_COUNTRY",
  "query": "nike air force 1",
  "countries": ["fr", "de", "es", "it", "nl"],
  "maxItems": 100,
  "sortBy": "newest_first"
}

Then use SELL_THROUGH_TRACKER for the countries that repeatedly look interesting. My rule:

CROSS_COUNTRY = find where to look
SELL_THROUGH_TRACKER = learn what moves
PRICE_TRACK = watch exact item URLs

🔔 Webhook payload I would actually send

If I schedule this every day, I do not want the full dataset in Telegram. I want a compact message:

{
  "workflow": "vinted-cross-country-radar",
  "query": "nike air force 1",
  "bestBuyCountry": "fr",
  "bestSellCountry": "de",
  "arbitrageSpreadPct": 47.4,
  "sampleSize": {
    "fr": 3,
    "de": 3,
    "es": 3,
    "it": 3,
    "nl": 3
  },
  "action": "open-sell-through-tracker-for-fr"
}

That is the difference between scraping and monitoring. Scraping gives you rows. Monitoring tells you what to do next.
